# Documentation and Content Style

## Core Principle: Concise and Factual Writing
All developer-facing content, documentation, landing page copy, guides, and
descriptions must be concise, factual, and easily digestible.

## Writing Guidelines
1. **Focus on Facts**:
   - Clearly state what an API, guide, demo, or feature actually does.
   - Use plain, standard technical terms that developers recognize immediately.

2. **Strictly Avoid Superlatives and Filler Adjectives**:
   - Do NOT use superlatives such as:
     - "high-performance"
     - "cutting-edge"
     - "production-grade"
     - "comprehensive"
     - "state-of-the-art"
     - "ultimate"
   - Do NOT use meaningless marketing adjectives or filler words such as:
     - "fully playable"
     - "real-world"
     - "vintage"
     - "essential"
     - "curated"

3. **Avoid Grandiose or Over-Engineered Phrasing**:
   - Replace buzzwords and bloated phrasing with simple equivalents:
     - Use "audio processors" instead of "DSP kernels".
     - Use "migration examples" instead of "architectural migration pathways".
     - Use "verification tests" instead of "interactive verification suites".

4. **Keep Content Readable and Scannable**:
   - Prefer short, direct sentences.
   - Keep introductory descriptions to 1–2 sentences maximum.

5. **High-Level Structural Scope Over Ephemeral Examples**:
   - Describe sections, categories, and landing page entries by their structural
     scope and architectural domain rather than citing narrow, ephemeral
     examples (e.g. avoid listing "drum machines, DJ decks" or "volume
     metering, error handling").
   - Keep descriptions evergreen so they remain accurate as individual
     samples, guides, and demos evolve over time.
